# Pricing & Packaging Recommendation - Roamly Groups

> Module 5 · Increase Revenue Through Smarter Pricing and Packaging · Deliverable 5

## 1. Pricing goal

- **Goal:** Maximize adoption and incremental marketplace GMV from an already-visible group-booking need, rather than monetize access to the Groups feature itself.
- **Why this is the right commercial goal now:** Roamly Groups is being introduced to fix conversion leakage in an existing marketplace. Charging travelers a new feature fee would add friction at exactly the point the product is supposed to remove it. The V1 commercial test should therefore be whether Groups creates more completed bookings, larger parties, and better contribution economics through Roamly's existing transaction model.

### Commercial principle

**Monetize the successful experience, not the coordination feature.**

The economic thesis is:

> **More completed group bookings × larger party size × existing marketplace take rate**
>
> minus
>
> **incremental payment processing + support + refund/cancellation cost**

If that contribution is attractive, Groups creates revenue without requiring a new consumer price.

## 2. Pricing model

- **Model:** Transaction-based, using Roamly's existing marketplace economics.
- **Traveler price for Groups:** **$0 incremental feature fee in V1.**
- **Who pays and for what:** Travelers pay the listed price of the experience. Hosts continue to fund marketplace monetization through Roamly's existing commission / take-rate structure on completed bookings. Groups should not introduce a separate organizer fee, participant fee, or subscription at launch.

### Why transaction-based fits the value

The value is realized only when the group successfully books an experience. Revenue should therefore scale with the transaction, not with access, seats, or time.

This also aligns with category economics:

- **Airbnb Experiences** currently deducts a **20% host service fee** from experience bookings.
- **GetYourGuide** operates an intermediary marketplace model and deducts supplier commission from successful bookings.
- **Viator** uses supplier-set net rates, with the marketplace controlling the retail shelf price while protecting the supplier's net payout.

Those benchmarks support a supplier-funded transaction model. They do **not** tell us what Roamly's exact take rate should be, so V1 should preserve Roamly's existing economics rather than invent a new commission.

## 3. Packaging approach

- **Structure:** One included Groups package for the beachhead use case, gated by group size and eligible inventory rather than by paid feature tiers.
- **Why:** The strategic bet is still unproven. Artificial Good / Better / Best packaging would create complexity before Roamly has established which group behaviors create incremental value.

| Package | Who it's for | Price | What's included |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Individual booking** | Solo travelers / existing Roamly flow | Existing experience price | Existing booking experience |
| **Roamly Groups ★** | Self-organized friend groups, approximately 3-8 travelers | **No incremental Groups fee** | One coordinated group booking, invitations, split payment, shared itinerary / participation visibility |
| **Large / managed groups** | 9+ travelers, corporate, events, travel planners | **Not a V1 offer** | Deliberately deferred until the consumer use case proves demand and economics |

### Packaging guardrail

Do not charge separately for the very behaviors needed to make PLG work:

- creating a group,
- inviting participants,
- joining a group,
- paying an individual share.

Those actions are the activation and distribution loop.

### What could become monetizable later

Only after V1 proves demand, Roamly could test paid value around **incremental service**, not basic coordination, for example:

- private / exclusive experience inventory,
- premium cancellation flexibility,
- concierge support,
- large-group planning,
- corporate or event workflows.

None of these should be assumed in the launch economics.

## 4. Research to validate

- **Method:** **Conjoint analysis**
- **Population:** The M3 beachhead ICP, organizers of self-organized leisure friend groups, with a secondary host sample for supply-side economics.
- **Assumption to validate:** Travelers value group payment and coordination enough to improve booking completion, but a separate Groups fee would reduce adoption more than the fee would add revenue.
- **The one thing that would change everything if wrong:** If Groups does **not** materially improve completed booking GMV, party size, or contribution margin, including it for free simply adds payment, support, and refund complexity without sufficient economic return.

### What the conjoint should trade off

For organizers:

- total experience price,
- separate Groups fee vs. no fee,
- ability for each traveler to pay independently,
- cancellation / refund flexibility,
- time allowed for the group to complete payment,
- private vs. shared experience options.

For hosts, separately validate:

- existing vs. higher marketplace take rate,
- expected group size,
- booking certainty,
- cancellation risk,
- payout timing.

The purpose is not to ask "would you pay for Groups?" in isolation. It is to understand which combination of price and group-booking value changes actual choice.

## 5. One-page recommendation

> **We recommend including Roamly Groups at no incremental traveler fee and monetizing completed group bookings through Roamly's existing transaction economics.** This supports the V1 goal of increasing group conversion and GMV without adding price friction to the product-led invite loop.

### What each stakeholder most needs to hear

- **Finance:** Do not evaluate Groups as a standalone feature SKU. Evaluate incremental contribution margin: conversion lift × party size × existing take rate, net of payment processing, refunds, support, and host-service costs. Full rollout should require positive incremental contribution economics.
- **Sales / Marketplace Leadership:** No new sales motion or supplier SKU is required for V1. Groups should make existing Roamly inventory easier to convert for small parties. Supplier teams should focus on eligible capacity, cancellation rules, and operational readiness.
- **Product Marketing:** The pricing story should stay invisible to the traveler: **"Everyone pays their share. No one carries the group."** Do not contaminate that promise with a new coordination fee.

### Validate first

**The single assumption:** Removing organizer payment burden increases completed group bookings enough to offset the incremental operational and payment-processing cost.

**How:** Run a controlled beta against the current booking flow and measure:

1. group-intent conversion,
2. completed booking GMV,
3. average party size,
4. payment completion,
5. cancellation / refund rate,
6. support contacts per booking,
7. contribution margin per group-intent session.

If conversion rises but contribution margin deteriorates, the pricing model is not yet proven.

## Pricing decision in one line

**Keep Groups free to use; earn more only when the group successfully books.**

## Market benchmark notes

- Airbnb Experiences: host service fee is typically 20% of the experience price.
- GetYourGuide: supplier commission is deducted from successful bookings under its intermediary marketplace model.
- Viator: suppliers set a net rate and Viator manages the retail shelf price.

These are category benchmarks, not recommended Roamly rates. Roamly's actual existing take rate is not provided in the scenario and should not be invented.
