## 2. The problem and the audience

- **Problem worth solving:** High-intent groups are trying to book Roamly experiences together, but the product still makes them coordinate like separate individuals. One person becomes the planner, scheduler, payment collector, and financial backstop.
- **Primary ICP:** The friend who ends up organizing a 3-8 person leisure activity while traveling.
- **What they do today, the alternative:** Group chat plus separate bookings plus Venmo, PayPal, or Splitwise plus one person manually coordinating everyone.

## 3. GTM motion

**Chosen motion:** Product-led growth.

Roamly Groups is a consumer, self-service capability layered onto an existing marketplace. The organizer can discover an experience, start a group, invite participants, and bring additional users into Roamly through normal product use. Community, reviews, social, creator content, and lifecycle messaging support acquisition, but the core motion is product-led.

### Why this is the right motion

**Acquire one organizer → organizer creates group → participants join → group books → invited travelers experience Roamly → some become future organizers**

This loop does not require a sales organization, a B2B buyer, or a new enterprise workflow.

- **Acquisition:** Reach organizers through Roamly's existing discovery surfaces and channels, then use group invitations as the participant acquisition mechanism.
- **Activation:** The first activation signal is that an organizer creates a group and at least one invited traveler joins. The stronger activation signal is a completed group booking with multiple participant payments.
- **Monetization:** No incremental Groups fee in V1. Monetize successful group bookings through Roamly's existing transaction economics.

## 4. V1 one-pager

### GTM thesis

> For the friend who ends up organizing a group's local activities and needs everyone to commit and pay without chasing the group or fronting the cost, Roamly Groups is a shared booking experience for curated local activities that gets everyone onto one coordinated reservation. Unlike stitching the experience together across chats, separate bookings, and payment apps, Roamly Groups lets each traveler join, commit, and pay their share while keeping the group on one booking.

### Strategy in one sentence

**Win the organizer, remove the coordination tax, and let one successful booking acquire the group.**

### What we are deliberately not doing in V1

- Not launching first for corporate offsites.
- Not building travel-agency or event-planner workflows.
- Not optimizing for large organized tours.
- Not adding a new feature fee that creates friction before the loop is proven.

## 5. Success metrics

| Metric | Target | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **North-star** | Statistically meaningful lift in group-intent booking conversion versus the existing flow | This proves Groups is solving a real conversion problem rather than adding a nice-to-have feature. |
| **Leading indicator** | Invite acceptance, participant join rate, payment completion, organizer-to-booking conversion, and time to completed booking | These show whether the PLG loop works before waiting for long-term retention. |
| **Business metric** | Higher completed booking GMV per group-intent session and positive incremental contribution margin | Confirms that the feature creates marketplace value after payment, refund, support, and host-service costs. |
| **Growth signal** | Invited travelers later return to Roamly or become organizers | Tests whether group invitations become customer acquisition, not just one-time payment participation. |
| **Guardrails** | No material increase in cancellation rate, payment failure, support contacts, host fulfillment issues, or time-to-book | Prevents a usability improvement from becoming an operational or economic drag. |

## 6. The assumption I am most worried about

**Requiring several travelers to participate in one booking will remove more coordination friction than it creates.**

This is the assumption that can break the strategy. If group completion depends on multiple people responding, joining, and paying, Roamly could accidentally create a new multi-person drop-off problem.

### How to test it

Run a controlled beta with users showing strong group intent and compare the Groups flow to the current workaround flow:

**Group intent → group created → invites sent → invites accepted → payments completed → booking completed → host fulfilled**

The bet is supported only if completed group bookings and contribution economics improve without material increases in support burden, cancellation, refund issues, or time-to-book.
