# Positioning Framework & Statement - Roamly Groups

> Module 3 · Master Product Positioning · Deliverable 3

## 1. Positioning framework, six components

| Component | Your answer |
|---|---|
| **Target customer - who is it for?** | The friend who ends up organizing a 3-8 person leisure activity while traveling. They find the activity, send the links, answer questions, track who is in, and often carry the payment burden. |
| **Market category - what is it?** | A shared booking experience for curated local activities. |
| **Key benefit - the number one value** | It gets the group from "we should do this" to booked and paid without making one person chase everyone or front the full cost. |
| **Competitive alternative - vs. what?** | Group chats, separate booking flows, payment apps, and one organizer manually stitching the experience together. For some travelers, Airbnb Experiences and other large marketplaces also compete for discovery and trip context. |
| **Differentiator - why us, uniquely?** | Roamly Groups is built around the commitment moment: each traveler can join, commit, and pay their share while staying on one coordinated reservation. |
| **Proof - evidence it is true** | Scenario proof: Roamly already sees group-booking workarounds and high drop-off. The marketplace has 12,000+ vetted local experts across 40 cities, and repeat booking is 3x the industry average. Peer feedback also confirmed that the organizer-payment burden was the clearest value signal. |

## 2. Core product truth

**Roamly Groups is not just a planning layer. It is a commitment layer.**

The product matters because it helps a group turn shared interest into one confirmed booking without making the organizer carry the money, reminders, and coordination alone.

## 3. Positioning statement

> **For the friend who ends up organizing the group's activities and needs everyone to commit and pay without chasing the group or fronting the cost, Roamly Groups is a shared booking experience for curated local activities that gets everyone onto one coordinated reservation. Unlike coordinating through group chats, separate bookings, and payment apps, Roamly Groups lets each traveler join, commit, and pay their share.**

## 4. Pressure tests

| Test | Pass? | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Ownable - could a competitor say the same?** | Mostly | A competitor could copy split payments, so the durable territory cannot be payment alone. The stronger claim is the full commitment workflow: organizer relief, individual responsibility, one reservation, and Roamly's curated local-expert marketplace. |
| **Believable - is the proof real?** | Yes, with limits | The scenario gives strong proof that the problem exists: workarounds, support signals, high drop-off, vetted experts, and high repeat booking. We should not claim conversion lift until the launch proves it. |
| **Relevant - does the ICP care?** | Yes | Peer feedback repeatedly surfaced the same value: do not make one organizer chase the group or front the money. That is specific, not generic group-travel convenience. |
| **Durable - holds as the product grows?** | Yes | The language can extend beyond friend groups later because the broader territory is group commitment. The V1 focus remains friend groups to keep the GTM sharp. |

## 5. Positioning vs. messaging note

The positioning is the stable strategic choice:

**Roamly Groups helps organizers turn group interest into one committed booking without carrying the group alone.**

Messaging should flex by surface:

- **Homepage / experience page:** needs to explain what Roamly Groups is first, then why it matters.
- **In-app organizer prompt:** can lead with relief because the user is already in the booking context.
- **Participant invite:** should lead with trust and ease: a friend invited you, your spot is clear, and you only pay your share.
- **Host message:** should lead with clearer committed demand, not traveler convenience.

### Customer-facing lines to carry forward

- **Umbrella:** Roamly Groups turns group interest into shared commitment.
- **Organizer:** From "we should do this" to booked.
- **Participant:** Your spot. Your share. One group booking.
- **Host:** One group. One booking. Less uncertainty.

## Blind-read learning

A peer cold read confirmed that the audience and value were clear. Readers identified the product as being for the person organizing a friend-group activity and understood the value as one coordinated booking where each person pays their own share.

The main refinement was to avoid overclaiming outcomes such as "nobody drops out." The final statement focuses on what Roamly Groups can credibly enable: joining, committing, paying, and staying on one coordinated reservation.
