# Launch Plan & Individual Insights - Roamly Groups

> Module 6 · Launch · Deliverable 6

## 1. Launch plan

| Phase | Goal | Key activities | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-launch** | Prove the group-booking flow removes more friction than it creates before broad exposure. | Identify high-intent group sessions; recruit organizers from the 3-8 friend-group ICP; validate eligible host capacity; instrument the full funnel; test invitations, split payments, refunds, reminders, and support flows; establish control-group baselines. | Product + Data, with Design, Payments, Support, Host Ops |
| **Launch** | Release to a controlled slice of Roamly demand and validate conversion, economics, and the PLG loop. | Surface Groups to likely organizers in-product; activate organizer, participant, and host messaging; monitor invite acceptance, payment completion, booking completion, GMV, support burden, cancellations, and contribution economics daily; collect qualitative feedback from organizers and hosts. | Product + Growth, with Lifecycle, Marketplace Ops, Data |
| **Post-launch** | Scale only what is working and decide whether the strategic bet deserves broader investment. | Compare beta vs. control; diagnose funnel drop-off; refine messaging and payment flow; expand eligible experiences and cities only where supply is healthy; test whether invited travelers return independently; decide whether to scale, iterate, or stop. | Product + Growth + Finance + Marketplace Leadership |

### Launch sequence

**Organizer sees group intent prompt → creates a Group → invites friends → participants join and pay → one booking completes → host fulfills → Roamly measures repeat / future organizer behavior**

The launch should stay deliberately narrow at first. Roamly is not trying to prove that every type of group needs the feature. It is trying to prove that the chosen beachhead, self-organized friend groups, converts better when the organizer no longer carries the full coordination and payment burden.

## 2. Launch metrics & guardrails

### Success looks like

**Primary outcome:** A statistically meaningful improvement in **group-intent booking conversion** versus the existing flow.

**Supporting business signals:**
- higher completed booking GMV per group-intent session,
- healthy average party size,
- positive incremental contribution margin after payment, refund, support, and host-service costs,
- meaningful invite acceptance and participant payment completion,
- evidence that invited travelers later return to Roamly or become organizers themselves.

**Customer / operational signals:**
- lower organizer coordination burden,
- no material deterioration in time-to-book,
- stable or improved cancellation / refund rates,
- manageable support contacts,
- reliable host fulfillment.

### Tripwire - when to pull back

Pause expansion if **Groups fails to improve completed group bookings or contribution economics while materially increasing any of the following:**

- payment failure,
- time-to-book,
- organizer support burden,
- cancellations / refunds,
- host coordination effort.

The most important failure mode is not low feature usage by itself. It is evidence that distributing commitment across several travelers creates more friction than the organizer-paid workaround.

## 3. The full GTM story in one slide

### Roamly Groups - the bet

**Observed behavior**  
Travelers already try to coordinate group experiences across separate accounts, chats, and payment tools, and these sessions show high drop-off.

↓  

**Beachhead**  
Self-organized leisure friend groups of approximately 3-8 adults.

↓  

**Beachhead user**  
The friend who ends up organizing the group's activities.

↓  

**Job to be done**  
Get everyone from **"we should do this" to booked and paid** without chasing the group or fronting the entire cost.

↓  

**Strategic bet**  
The remaining pain in small-group experience booking is **commitment**, not simply discovery: getting several independent travelers onto one reservation without forcing one organizer to carry the coordination and money risk.

↓  

**Positioning**  
Roamly Groups is a shared booking experience for curated local activities where each traveler can join, commit, and pay their own share inside one coordinated reservation.

↓  

**GTM motion**  
**Product-led growth:** win one organizer, let the organizer bring the group.

↓  

**Messaging**  
**Roamly Groups turns group interest into shared commitment.**

Organizer: **From "we should do this" to booked.**

↓  

**Pricing**  
**No incremental Groups fee in V1. Monetize the successful experience, not the coordination feature.**

↓  

**Proof test**  
If conversion, GMV, and contribution economics improve without increasing operational friction, scale the loop.

### One-line GTM thesis

> **Win the organizer, remove the coordination tax, and let one successful booking acquire the group.**

## 4. Individual insights

### Biggest "aha" across the certification

**A GTM strategy gets stronger every time you remove something.**

The work improved when the audience narrowed from "group travelers" to small friend groups, then again when the user narrowed to the organizer, and again when the differentiation moved from generic collaboration to the specific commitment moment.

The important choices were as much about **what not to pursue** as what to pursue.

### What changed in how I think about GTM

I started with the instinct to describe the product and its capabilities. The stronger approach is to build the chain in order:

**person → problem → alternative → differentiated value → motion → message → economics → proof**

If any one link is weak, launch activity cannot compensate for it.

I also came away with a clearer distinction between **positioning and messaging**. Positioning is the strategic choice about who the product is for and why it wins. Messaging changes by audience and surface without changing that underlying choice.

### What I would do differently with more time / data

I would spend less time polishing messaging early and more time validating the two assumptions that can break the entire strategy:

1. **Is coordination / payment friction actually a material cause of group-session abandonment?**
2. **Does distributing commitment across multiple travelers remove more friction than it introduces?**

I would also want Roamly's actual group-intent funnel, take rate, contribution margin, cancellation data, support contacts, and host capacity before setting numeric launch thresholds or changing marketplace pricing.

### The one idea I will apply at work on Monday

**Turn the riskiest assumption into an explicit test before scaling the solution.**

Instead of asking, "How do we launch this?" start with:

> **What has to be true for this strategy to work, and what is the fastest credible way to prove or disprove it?**

That is the discipline I would carry forward.
