## 1. The competitive set

The real competitive set is not just other experience marketplaces. Roamly Groups competes against both scaled travel platforms and the current do-it-yourself workaround.

| Competitor | What they do well | Where they are weak | How we win |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Airbnb Experiences** | Massive traveler base, strong trip context, homes plus experiences, shared trip planning, group messages, and brand trust. | Strong at planning and discovery, but less clearly built around distributed commitment and payment for several independent travelers inside one experience booking. | Own the commitment moment: one reservation where every traveler can join, commit, and pay their share without one organizer carrying the group. |
| **GetYourGuide** | Scaled experiences marketplace, strong supply, reviews, discovery, and booking utility. | Primarily optimized for booking experiences, not for turning a friend group's interest into shared commitment. | Be more group-native for small friend groups, with invitations, individual payment, and shared booking visibility. |
| **Viator** | Large inventory, broad destination coverage, reviews, and transactional confidence. | Strong marketplace utility, but the group still often coordinates outside the product. | Replace the off-platform coordination stack with one Roamly group booking. |
| **DIY workaround: group chat + separate bookings + Venmo / Splitwise** | Familiar, flexible, already used by customers. No switching barrier. | Puts the burden on one organizer, fragments the booking, creates repayment friction, and increases drop-off. | Make the current workaround unnecessary by keeping the people, payment, and booking connected. |

## 2. Battlecard

### Primary competitor

**Airbnb Experiences**

This is the strongest strategic competitor because Airbnb can attack the same customer moment from inside a broader trip ecosystem. If the group is already using Airbnb for the trip, Airbnb has the advantage of context, trust, identity, and distribution.

### Our one-line advantage

> **Airbnb helps friends plan parts of the trip. Roamly Groups helps them commit to one experience without making one friend carry the booking and payment burden.**

### Trap to set / question to plant

> **Who has to put the whole group experience on their card?**

That question exposes the difference between collaborative planning and group-native commitment.

### Objection → response

- **"Everyone already uses Airbnb. Why would our group use Roamly?"** → Because the problem Roamly Groups solves is not just finding an experience. It is getting several independent travelers committed and paid into one coordinated reservation without one person chasing the group.
- **"Could Airbnb or another marketplace copy split payments?"** → Yes. Split payment alone is not the moat. Roamly has to win by becoming excellent at the full commitment workflow: invitations, participant status, payment completion, organizer relief, booking integrity, and host fulfillment.
- **"Is this just another planning feature?"** → No. The strategic focus is not shared planning. It is shared commitment. The product has to move the group from interest to a booked experience.

## 3. The strategic bet

> **We are betting that the remaining unsolved pain in small-group experience booking is commitment, not discovery: getting several independent travelers onto one reservation without forcing one organizer to carry the coordination and money risk.**

### Why now

Roamly already sees users trying to coordinate group bookings through workarounds, and Support has flagged the pattern. Group-oriented sessions also show high drop-off. That means the need is already visible inside Roamly's own funnel, not just in market research.

### What we are deliberately not doing

- We are not competing head-on to own the entire trip itinerary.
- We are not launching as a corporate offsite or event-planning product.
- We are not building travel-agency workflows.
- We are not claiming shared planning is the moat.
- We are not adding a paid Groups SKU before the conversion and economics are proven.

### What would prove the bet right within 90 days

The bet is right if Roamly Groups demonstrates that distributed commitment and payment materially improves completed group bookings versus the current flow, without increasing time-to-book, support burden, payment failures, cancellations, or host coordination issues.

## 4. Evidence

- Roamly already sees users coordinating group bookings across separate accounts, splitting payments manually, and syncing availability outside the product.
- Group-oriented sessions currently show high drop-off.
- Roamly has 12,000+ vetted local experts across 40 cities, giving the product enough supply depth to test the use case.
- Roamly repeat booking is 3x the industry average, suggesting strong existing product affinity.
- Peer blind reads consistently understood the core pain as: one organizer should not have to chase the group or front the cost.

## Strategic insight

**Airbnb is built around the trip. Roamly Groups is positioning around the moment the group commits.**

This keeps the strategy narrow and defensible. Roamly does not need to out-Airbnb Airbnb. It needs to be the better answer when a group is ready to turn a local experience from an idea into a paid reservation.
